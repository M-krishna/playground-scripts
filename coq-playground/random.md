# Learning things on the go

## What is Proposition in Mathematics?
In mathematics, a proposition is a statement that is **either true or false**.

Examples:
* "2 + 2 = 4" -> true proposition
* "7 is an even number" -> false proposition

Both are propositions because they have a definite truth value.

### Important Distinction
A proposition is **not**:
* a question
* a command
* an ambiguous sentence

Examples that are **not propositions:**
* "What time is it?"
* "Close the door"
* "This movie is great". (subjective/vague)

These don't have a clear true/false value.

### In Logic
Propositions are building blocks of **mathematical logic**. You can combine them using logical operators:
| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| ¬P | NOT P |
| P ^ Q | P AND Q |
| P ∨ Q | P OR Q |
| P -> Q | If P then Q |
| p <-> Q | P iff Q |

Example:
* P: "It is raining".
* Q: "The ground is wet".

Then:
* P -> Q means "if it is raining, then the ground is wet."

### In Mathematical writing
The word "proposition" also has a second meaning. In textbooks/papers, a **Proposition** is a named mathematical result that has been proven.

Hierarchy usually looks like:
1. **Lemma** -> helper result
2. **Proposition** -> important result
3. **Theorem** -> major result
4. **Corollary** -> direct consequence of another result

Example:
> **Proposition**: Even even integer greater than 2 can be written as the sum of two primes. (not proven)

A proven proposition might look like:
> **Proposition:** The sum of two even numbers is even.

Then a proof follows.

### Simple Intuition
Think of a proposition as:
> "A sentence that mathematics can judge."

Either mathematics says:
* true
or
* false
